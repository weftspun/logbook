Four Loops in Normal Form

Four pipelines propose something, score it against whatever conditioned it, repair, and go round again. They share one shape, so they share one schema. What follows is every input the four take, written as relations: interned vocabularies, satellites instead of nullable columns, no NULLs, and nothing stored that can be computed. The interesting half is what is deliberately absent.

The four rules, and where each one bites

Essential Tuple Normal Form is stated in four clauses, and each of them changed a specific column here rather than being satisfied in the abstract.

RuleWhere it bitWhat it would have been
interned vocabularies instructions is a relation, and a score carries an instruction_id. The instruction text repeated on every score row, which is how two rounds end up claiming to answer the same question in two slightly different wordings.
satellites, not nullable columns Three generator satellites. OmniGen2 has steps and two guidance scales, CycleGAN has a style and a checkpoint hash, Pixal3D has a seed and a view count. One generations table eight columns wide, five of them null on every row, and no way to tell "not applicable" from "not recorded".
no NULLs A refused score writes no scores tuple. A referee region that did not run writes no fit_residuals tuple, which is NOT_RUN. repaired_from is −1 on a first round.A value like −1 for "no parent" is a value. A NULL is not. A null overall, which enters an average as a zero and turns a refusal into a measurement of failure rather than the absence of a measurement.
no derivable columns view_scores stores hammersley_index and view_count and no camera at all. Yaw, pitch, radius and eye stored beside the index, free to disagree with the sequence that produced them the first time anybody changes the view count.

The router is a relation, not a branch

Loop 4 chooses between editing the latent and editing a view, and the choice is recorded rather than implied by which artifact appeared next. arm_selections holds run_id, round_index, arm_id and the two numbers the choice was made on. High variance across views means the views disagree about the shape, and a shape wrong along one axis scores well from the view that hides it, so a single hand-picked front view cannot separate that case from a flat appearance failure.

One arm cannot run, and the schema says so

VoxHammer's server dispatches its seven-step plan and raises NotImplementedError on every step outside stub mode. It also takes a mesh rather than the latent, so the arm passes through /extract first.

arm_unavailable is a satellite on arm_selections: a tuple exists when the router picked an arm that could not execute. Falling through to the other arm would leave no trace at all, and a geometry failure would be recorded as an appearance failure that was repaired.

Two counts that are not interchangeable

RF-DETR emits 17 joints and ANNY's coco.pth emits 23, the extra six being foot points. They are separate vocabularies with a mapping relation between them, because a shared name would let a 23-row array reach a 17-row consumer and be silently truncated at the tail, which is exactly where the feet are.

The rendering side has the same shape of hazard. render_corpus.py requires the 19,158-vertex makehuman topology; the corpus builder's body submodel is 13,718 vertices and the keypoint weights are indexed against the first. One direction fails loudly. The other does not, and a topology_id foreign key on any vertex-indexed relation is what keeps it from being a guess.
